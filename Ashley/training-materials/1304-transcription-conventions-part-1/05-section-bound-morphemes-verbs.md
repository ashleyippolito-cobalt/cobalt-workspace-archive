# Section: Bound Morphemes: Verbs

Section outline

Verbs

/3S	3rd Person Singular verb form. Examples: go/3s, tell/3s
Do not mark irregular forms. Examples: has, was
Do not mark irregulars when the sound of the root changes. Examples: do --> does, say --> says
/ED	Past tense. Examples: love/ed, die/ed
Do not mark irregular form. Examples: did, grew, had, sank
Do not mark predicate adjectives. Examples: was tired, is bored
Transcription hint: regular past tense /ED verbs never follow a BE or GET verb.

/EN	Past participle. Examples: take/en, eat/en, prove/en
Do not mark irregular forms. Examples: gotten, spoken, seen, been
Do not mark irregulars when the sound of the root changes. Example: write --> written
Transcription hint: Regular form: present tense + EN as separate syllable. /EN verbs always follow the HAVE verb.

/ING	Verb inflection. Examples: go/ing, run/ing, bike/ing
Do not mark gerunds. Examples: went swimming, reading is fun


Verbs Ending With -ING

The progressive verb form indicates ongoing action and is formed by combining the verb "to be" or the verb "keep" with the -ing form of a verb, e.g., He is read/ing. They were read/ing. The book is be/ing read. He will be leave/ing. I have been sleep/ing. Keep walk/ing. She kept run/ing. The /ING is added to the verb stem without changing the spelling of the stem word, e.g., The dog is swim/ing.

Gerunds

Gerunds, which are verb forms that function as nouns, are sometimes confused with progressive verb forms because they both end with -ing. In SALT transcription, the “ing at the end of the gerund is NOT slashed as a bound morpheme. Gerunds can function as subjects, objects, or complements and sometimes end in -ing or -ed. In the following examples, the gerund is bolded and the verb is underlined.

Gerunds as Subject, e.g., Biking is a great way to exercise. Traveling is so much fun.

Gerunds as Direct object (a direct object is the part of speech that answers "what" or "whom"): e.g., I don't like writing letter/s. They went swimming. My favorite activity is reading book/s. These shirt/s need washing. Hint #1: if you can replace the -ing form with the infinitive form (to write, to swim, to read, to be washed), without changing the meaning, the -ing form is a gerund.

Other gerunds as direct objects: They enjoy playing basketball. She avoid/3s running. She is consider/ing going out. My sister is annoying. Hint #2: if the -ing form answers the question "what" (They enjoy what? She avoids what? She is considering what?, My sister is what?), the -ing form is a gerund. Note that if the last example was "My sister is annoy/ing me", the -ing form is a present progressive verb

Gerunds as Object after Preposition (prepositions are words that show the relationship between a noun/pronoun and some other word or element in the rest of the sentence), e.g., She insist/ed on calling him. I had gym before going to math class. Hint: if you see an -ing verb after a preposition, it must be a gerund.

Preposition list: aboard, about, above, across, after, against, ahead of, along, amid, amidst, among, around, as, as far as, as of, aside from, at, atop, barring, because of, before, behind, below, beneath, beside, besides, between, beyond, but, by, by means of, circa, concerning, despite, down, during, except, except for, excluding, far from, following, for, from, in, in accordance with, in addition to, in case of, in front of, in lieu of, in place of, in spite of, inside, instead of, into, like, minus, near, next to, of, off, on, on account of, on behalf of, on top of, onto, opposite, out, out of, outside, over, past, plus, prior to, regarding, regardless of, save, since, than, through, till, to, toward, towards, under, underneath, unlike, until, up, upon, versus, via, with, with regard to, within, without.

Gerunds as Complement (a complement is a noun, pronoun, or adjective following a direct object), e.g., I found the guard sleeping. We consider the fish spoiled once it smell/3s like fish.

Verbs Ending With -ED

Use the bound morpheme /ED to mark regular past tense verbs. See examples below of the different forms of past tense verbs.

Simple Past Tense, e.g., He said that if they ever disrespect/ed each other he would want them to run. They look/ed for the frog.

Present perfect, e.g., I have walk/ed home. They have look/ed everywhere.

Past perfect, e.g., I had walk/ed home. He had look/ed everywhere.

Conditional perfect, e.g., I would have walk/ed home. I would have talk/ed to the coach.

Future perfect, e.g., I will have walk/ed home. They will have arrive/ed by eight.

Past Participles, Passive Forms, and Predicate Adjectives

These forms, which end with -ed, are sometimes confused with past tense verbs. However they are not verb forms and the -ed at the end of these words are NOT slashed as bound morphemes. Examples follow.

Past participles, e.g., Her hair was well brushed. She was tired. The porcupine was named Fluffy.

Passive Forms, e.g., The store was robbed last night. The vase is smashed.

Predicate adjectives, e.g., The grape/s became shriveled in the sun. They seem/ed irritated by the kid/s. I like car/s produced in Japan.
