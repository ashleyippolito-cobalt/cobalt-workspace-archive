Collapse
Case Study/Demo Video

SALT allows users to link any two transcripts together for analysis. Once the samples are linked, reports generated from the Analyze and Database menus display language measures for both of the linked samples in a side-by-side comparison. Linking can be used, for example, to compare pre and post therapy (i.e., document response to intervention), performance in different languages (i.e.,Spanish and English), and performance in different sampling contexts (i.e., narrative and conversation).

Practice Exercises

In this section, you will link samples on your own and answer questions to monitor your understanding.

About the samples. Two samples were collected from Dan, the first when he was 11;2 and then again six months later when Dan was 11;8. Dan is diagnosed with Autism and ADHD and falls on severe end of the Autism Spectrum. He has limited language skills. Dan uses oral language to communicate but has difficulty interacting with peers, responding to questions, and staying on topic. He tends to perseverate on topics and is difficult to redirect back to his work. He has been working on responding appropriately to questions in therapy. In these conversational samples, the traditional SALT elicitation protocol for conversation was not used since Dan is not able to participate effectively in a conversational sample or story retell. This sample focused around a game where dice with pictures on them were used to simulate language. Since response to questions is the area of interest with Dan, a plethora of questions were asked when eliciting his language samples.

Summary

By linking Dan's samples, you were able to look at specific features of Dan's language and his progress over time with convenient side-by-side reports. The Standard Measures Report showed that Dan's overall language measures such as MLU, number of different words, and utterance level errors did not change much from time one to time two.

Linking can also be used to compare the speaker's performance across languages, e.g., Spanish and English, and in different speaking contexts, e.g., conversation and narrative. Once samples are linked, generate any of the reports from the Analyze and Database menus.
