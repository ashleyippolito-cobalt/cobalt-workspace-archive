Course 1504: PSS - Persuasion Scoring Scheme
Collapse all

The Persuasion Scoring Scheme (PSS) assesses the structure of persuasive argument, a critical language skill in secondary curriculum, using a scoring rubric consisting of the essential characteristics of a coherent persuasive argument. Learn how to score language transcripts for PSS using SALT and how to compare PSS scores from an individual sample to scores from samples selected from the Persuasion reference database.

Materials Needed:

Any copy of SALT 24 or SALT 20 software. Older versions of the software may be used though you may notice differences in the menu structures and the reports.
Adobe Reader® or other PDF reader which is needed to view document files.

PSS Scoring Guide

The PSS is scored on the following categories:1) Issue identification and desired change, 2) Supporting reasons, 3) Other point of view, 4) Compromises, 5) Conclusion, 6) Cohesion, and 7) Effectiveness. The first five of these categories correspond to the Persuasion Planning Sheet used when eliciting the persuasion sample.

Read the PSS Scoring Guide which contains detailed instructions for scoring transcripts.

Practice Samples Description

There are five transcripts for you to use as practice. You should 1) download each transcript, 2) score it for PSS using the PSS Scoring Guide, and 3) compare your scores to our scored transcripts and read through the reasoning for the score.  The master transcript is highlighted to point out the sections of the transcript that support the assigned score for each PSS characteristic.
