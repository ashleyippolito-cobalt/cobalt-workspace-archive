Max's Expository Sample

In this section, you will analyze Max's expository sample on your own and answer questions to monitor your understanding.

Max is a 5th grader who has received speech and language services since age four.  He was identified as having a learning disability when he was in first grade. In therapy Max has been working on word retrieval, thought organization, and staying on topic. His clinician elicited an expository language sample from Max using the SALT expository elicitation protocol.

You will need the software to complete this section. You may find it helpful to print out the reports to refer to while completing the Self Check questions.

Practice - Part 1b
Re-run the database Standard Measures Report.

In the previous section, you generated the Standard Measures Report comparing Max's entire expository sample to entire samples selected from the database. In this section, you will re-run the report except this time you will equate all the samples (Max's and the database samples) so that they are the same length, with the longer samples being cut to match the length of the shortest sample. Equating the samples by some measure of length evens the playing field providing a more apples-to-apples comparison for language measures affected by length. For example, shorter samples may reduce the opportunity to produce more and different words, mazes, and/or errors. And, conversely, longer samples may increase the opportunity to produce them. You may recall that, when you selected the database samples in the previous section, you kept the default of equating the samples by the same number of total words (NTW). As it turns out, Max's sample is relatively short so all the selected database samples would be cut to match the length of Max's sample.

But let's suppose, for the sake of discussion, that Max's sample was significantly longer than the database samples. In that case, all the samples, including Max's, would be cut to match the length of the shortest database sample. And because the end of Max's sample would be cut off, some of the language produced in his sample would not be included in the report. This may or may not be important, depending on how different the end of his sample is from the rest of his sample. If this was a concern, you would generate the report both ways (based on the entire transcript and equated by length) and compare the results.

SALT 20

Re-run the report by selecting Database menu --> Standard Measures Report.

When prompted to choose what the report is based on, select Equated by length and click OK.



SALT 18

The database Standard Measures Report includes a top section with language measures always calculated on the entire sample and a bottom section with language measures based on the setting specified in STEP 3 where you select the database samples to use for comparison.

Change the database comparison set using the Database menu --> Change Database Samples and Settings option.
Keep the age match at plus or minus 6 months.

Click Find Equated Samples and select the first option 83 samples - 252 words. Click OK.

Practice - Part 1e
SALT 24: Generate the database Quick Look.
Select Database menu --> Quick Look. 
When presented with the "Report Based On" dialogue box, select the Entire transcript and click OK. The Quick Look report is displayed.
The Quick Look includes a set of measures selected from the Standard Measures Report and indicates which measures show strengths, which show weaknesses, and which are within normal limits (WNL).

SALT 24 and SALT 20: Generate the database Performance Report.
Select Database menu --> Performance Report. 
When presented with the "Performance Report Settings" dialogue box, notice that the name (Max) and gender (M) are selected based on the values of the +Name and +Gender plus lines in the transcript. Click OK and the Performance Report is displayed.
The Performance Report is narrative summary of language samples generated using the Standard Measures Report and supplemental reports. It can be generated using a database comparison (Database menu) or without a database comparison (Analyze menu). Contents from the Performance Report can be copied and pasted into a document or printed as a PDF.
Look through this report. Notice that text from the report can be edited to suit individual preferences, e.g., add your clinical impressions, relate results to other information. The report can be copied and pasted into other applications such as an electronic record keeping software or a word processing document.

Summary

This course demonstrated the process of SALT analysis. A breakdown of the key steps of the analysis process was provided and then put into practice using a case study, directed exercises, and practice exercises. The final set of practice exercises, where you viewed the Standard Measures Reports of four different speakers, showed how SALT can be used to summarize a variety of language patterns. The Performance Report, which provides a narrative of the results, can be edited and copied into your reports. The vast amount of data SALT generates will be invaluable to your clinical work and the Performance Report narrative will save you considerable time when writing your reports.
