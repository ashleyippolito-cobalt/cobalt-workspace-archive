# Section: Bilingual Unique Story Task

<!-- https://classes.saltsoftware.com/course/section.php?id=216 -->

Section outline

With the unique story task, the student looks through the pictures in a book and is then asked to tell the story. No model of this story is given. It is assumed, however, that the student has previously produced a story retell and is familiar with the notion of telling a story based on the pictures. If not, the examiner should begin this task by modeling a different frog story.

The SALT unique story database contains both English and Spanish samples from almost 500 English language learners in kindergarten through 3rd grade using the wordless picture book One Frog Too Many by Mercer and Marianna Mayer (1975).

Read the Bilingual SE Unique Story database description and the elicitation protocol. 

Elicitation Method

Option 1 (In-person or virtual session): Use SALT's Online Story Elicitation Bilingual Spanish/English & Monolingual Spanish program. Select the category (Bilingual Spanish/English), the target language (Spanish or English), and the story (One Frog Too Many). Follow the prompts. There is a YouTube video showing how to elicit a Spanish sample in the Summary section of this course. 

Option 2 (In-person session): Have the speaker seated next to you with the book in front of you. Have the speaker quietly look through the pictures of the book. Then ask the speaker to use the book to retell the story in his or her own words.
