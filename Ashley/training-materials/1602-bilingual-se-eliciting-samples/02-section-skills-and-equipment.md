# Section: Skills and Equipment

Section outline

What skills are needed to elicit the samples?

One doesn't have to be a speech language pathologist to elicit a language sample. The examiner should, however, have experience talking with children. The first few minutes of the language sample interaction are critical. If the examiner fails to establish a comfortable rapport with the speaker, the resulting language sample will be strained and will lack the necessary spontaneity to function as a valid index of the speaker's productive language performance. View eliciting good language samples for suggestions.

There are different ways to provide the story model and record the student's story. You may have the student seated next to you or you may be eliciting the sample virtually. You may be using SALT's Online Story Elicitation program (available as an annual subscription on the SALT web site) or providing the directions and story model yourself. Both methods should result in similar outcomes (Manning, et al., 2020, Brennan, et al., 2004). 

If you are providing the directions and story model yourself, you should be proficient in the target language (Spanish or English). Otherwise, you should be proficient enough in the target language to be an engaged listener and to prompt the child as needed. 

What equipment is needed?

The equipment needed depends on the method you are using to elicit the samples.

Option 1: Use SALT's Online Story Elicitation Bilingual Spanish/English & Monolingual Spanish program (in-person or virtual session).

If this in an in-person session, use the recorder built into the program. If eliciting the sample virtually, use an alternate recorder, e.g., your phone, the recorder built into Zoom or other software for hosting virtual meetings. All directions and story images are included in the program.

Option 2: Provide the directions and story models yourself (in-person session).

Equipment list:

An audio or video recorder. Use a digital recorder for better sound quality and more playback options.
The specific story book used for the elicitation.
The elicitation protocol and story scripts.
Audio recording of the Spanish and English scripts (alternate elicitation protocol). They are available from the Resources section of the SALT web site.
