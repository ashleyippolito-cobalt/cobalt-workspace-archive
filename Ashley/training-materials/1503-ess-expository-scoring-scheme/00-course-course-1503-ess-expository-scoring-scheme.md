Course 1503: ESS - Expository Scoring Scheme
Collapse all

The Expository Scoring Scheme (ESS) assesses the structure of expository language, a critical language skill in secondary curriculum (ages 10 and up), using a scoring rubric consisting of the essential characteristics of a coherent expository. Learn how to score language transcripts for ESS using SALT and how to compare ESS scores from an individual sample to scores from samples selected from the reference databases.

Lecture Introducing ESS

The Expository Scoring Scheme assesses the structure of expository language, a critical language skill in secondary curriculum, using a scoring rubric consisting of the essential characteristics of a coherent expository. These characteristics include: 1) object, 2) preparations, 3) start, 4) course of play, 5) rules, 6) scoring, 7) duration, 8) strategies, 9) terminology, and 10) cohesion.

ESS Scoring Guide

The ESS is scored on the following ten categories: 1) Object of Contest, 2) Preparations, 3) Start of Play, 4) Course of Play, 5) Rules, 6) Scoring, 7) Duration, 8) Strategy, 9) Terminology, and 10) Cohesion. The first eight of these categories correspond to the Expository Planning Sheet used when eliciting the expository sample.

Read the ESS Scoring Guide which contains detailed instructions for scoring transcripts.
