# Section: Mazes and Part Words

<!-- https://classes.saltsoftware.com/course/section.php?id=248 -->

Section outline

Mazes are  filled pauses, repetitions, and revisions. Mazes can indicate word finding or utterance formulation difficulties. Some rules for marking mazes follow:

Enclose the maze in parentheses (  ).
Mark the earliest occurrence of the maze.
Combine sequential mazes.
Include pauses which occur immediately before, in the middle of, or immediately after, the mazed words.
End all part words with an asterisk (*).



For example, "We went to (um the :03) the store." Notice that the first production of "the" was mazed. The final word spoken is not marked as part of the maze. The filled pause and the repetition were included in the same maze and the pause immediately following the maze was also included.

Filled pauses: The default filled pause words include: AH, EH, ER, HM, HMM, MM, UH, and UM. Examples: "We went to the (um um) grocery store."  and "It/'s his (bir* er) birthday party."

Repetitions: To determine if a maze is a repetition SALT looks at what follows the maze. So it is important to mark the first occurring word(s) in the repetition as a maze. Examples: "She/'s (my my) my Aunt_Katie," and "(It/'s not um) it/'s not bad."

Revisions: If a maze component is not a filled pause or repetition then, by default, SALT considers it to be a revision. A speaker revises an utterance by choosing different words with the same semantic intent. Examples: "And then (I went) we went to the park." and "We (were) were/n't here."

Part words: Part words end with an asterisk (*) indicating that the rest of the word was omitted. Examples: "(We were go*) we went to the park." and "He (m* m*) made it."
