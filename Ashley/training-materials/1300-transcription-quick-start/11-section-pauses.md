# Section: Pauses

<!-- https://classes.saltsoftware.com/course/section.php?id=249 -->

Section outline

Marking pauses within a transcript can give clinicians valuable information about verbal rate and facility. Pauses can be telling of word finding, utterance formulation, or processing difficulties and are marked when they occur within an utterance or between an utterance. Pauses can be marked within and between utterances. All pauses of two seconds or longer were marked in the SALT database samples. Pauses do not have to be marked if this is not an area of interest. And, if marked, they do not have to be timed; they can be marked using just the semicolon or colon.

Pause times

If timed, mark the length of the pause in minutes:seconds or in :seconds. Examples:

Two-second pauses	

 :02

 0:02

 00:02


One-minute, two-second pause	

 01:02

 
Within-utterances pauses

Enter the pause where it occurs in the utterance. Separate the pause from the adjacent words with spaces. If the pause occurs immediately before or after a maze, include it in the maze. If the pause occurs in the middle of a word, use underscores to separate the part of the word preceding the pause from the part of the word following the pause. Examples:

C I went :02 sledding.




C I (went :02) went sledding.




C I went sled_ :02 _ding.

Between-utterance pauses

Colons (:) are used at the beginning of the utterance line to denote a change in speaker turn. Semicolons (;) are used to denote that there is not a change in speaker turn. However, if the speakers change, e.g., the pause occurs between the examiner's question and the student's response, then SALT recognizes a change in speaker turn regardless of whether the pause line begins with a colon or a semicolon. Examples:

Pause with a change in speaker turn (and a change in speaker).

Format options
    : :sec
    :sec
    : min:sec
    :min:sec

: :04
