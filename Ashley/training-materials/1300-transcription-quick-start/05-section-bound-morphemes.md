# Section: Bound Morphemes

<!-- https://classes.saltsoftware.com/course/section.php?id=246 -->

Section outline

Bound morphemes are an essential transcription convention. Grammatical morphemes are marked to measure the language development and complexity of young speakers. A specific set of inflectional morphemes are marked, but derivational morphemes are not marked (see this SALT blog for a detailed explanation). Bound morphemes are marked in SALT transcripts using a slash (/) to separate bound morphemes from free morphemes. Most of the bound morphemes are straight forward and easily recognized; however, several of them can be tricky when first learning to transcribe. See the tips below for more clarification. 

Bound Morphemes

/S

	

Plural

Examples: KITTEN/S, BABY/S

Do not mark words that end in "s" but represent one entity (e.g., PANTS, GLASSES). Do not mark irregular forms (e.g., MICE, GEESE, DEER) or when the sound of the root changes (e.g., LEAF --> LEAVES, WOLF --> WOLVES).




/Z

	

Possessive inflection

Examples: DAD/Z, MARY/Z.

Do not mark any possessive pronouns. Examples: MINE, HIS, HERS, OURS, YOURS, ITS, THEIRS




/S/Z

	

Plural and Possessive

Example: BABY/S/Z




/ED

	

Past tense

Examples: LOVE/ED, DIE/ED

Do not mark predicate adjectives (e.g., WAS TIRED, IS BORED).

Transcription hint: regular past tense /ED verbs will not follow a BE or GET verb.




/EN

	

Past participle

Examples: EAT/EN, TAKE/EN, PROVE/EN, 

Do not mark irregular forms (e.g., GOTTEN, SPOKEN, SEEN, BEEN) or when the sound of the root changes (e.g., WRITE --> WRITTEN). 

Transcription hint: Regular form: present tense + EN as separate syllable. /EN verbs always follow HAVE, HAS, or HAD.




/3S

	

3rd Person Singular verb form

Examples: GO/3S, TELL/3S, TRY/3S

Do not mark Irregular forms (e.g., HAS, WAS) or when the sound of the root changes (e.g., DO --> DOES, SAY --> SAYS).




/ING

	

Progressive verb form

Examples: GO/ING, RUN/ING, BIKE/ING

Do not mark the gerund use of the verb form (e.g., WENT SWIMMING, READING IS FUN).




/N'T, /'T

	

Negative contractions

Examples: CAN/'T, DOES/N'T

Do not mark irregular forms (e.g., WON'T) or when the sound of the root changes (e.g., DO --> DON'T). Do not mark the word AIN'T.




/'S, /'RE, /'M, /'LL, /'D, /'VE

	

Contracted --> IS, ARE, AM, WILL, WOULD, and HAVE

Examples: HE/'S, WE/'RE, I/'M, I/'LL, I/'D, WE/'VE




/H'S, /H'D, /D'S, /D'D, /'US

	

Contracted --> HAS, HAD, DOES, DID, and US.

Examples: HE/H'S been sick. WHAT/D'S he do for a living? Why/D'D the boy look for the frog? LET/'US go.

Tips

/ING versus Gerund

Recognizing gerunds can be confusing when transcribing. There is a tendency to mark all -ING endings, making them verb forms. However, many times words that end with -ing are not actually verbs; they are gerunds. Gerunds are verb forms that function as nouns, i.e., subjects, direct objects, indirect objects, and objects of prepositions, etc.

Gerunds

	

Verb Forms




C I went shopping.

C We went to the shopping mall.

C She start/ed bleeding.

C I/'m a singing machine.

	

C I am look/ing at it.

C I was try/ing.

C We will be go/ing.

C Just keep walk/ing.

/ED versus Predicate Adjective

Only mark the simple past tense verbs. Do not mark predicate adjective. Recognizing predicate adjectives can also be confusing when transcribing. A predicate adjective is an adjective that follows a linking verb and tells us something about the subject. If an ED verb follows a BE verb or a GET verb, it is a predicate adjective and should not be marked. 

Predicate Adjectives

	

Verb Forms




She/'s bored.

It get/3s congested.

He was tired.

	

He look/ed under the branch.

We play/ed outside.

It rain/ed yesterday.
