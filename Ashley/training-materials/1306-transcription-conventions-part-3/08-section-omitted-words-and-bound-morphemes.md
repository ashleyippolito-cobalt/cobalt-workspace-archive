# Section: Omitted Words and Bound Morphemes

<!-- https://classes.saltsoftware.com/course/section.php?id=94 -->

Section outline

You should mark omissions because they may be an indication of utterance formulation problems. An omission occurs when one or more words or bound morphemes that are obligatory for grammatical correctness are absent. For example, if the speaker says, "His name Larry", the copula "is" should be marked as an omission. On the other hand, if the examiner asks, "What are you doing?" and the speaker responds "Eating", there isn't an omission because no obligatory content has been left out.

Once marked, omissions can be counted and called up later for further analysis. They are not included in calculations such as "mean length of utterance", "number of different words", or "words per minute".

Omitted Words

The asterisk symbol (*) is used to indicate an omitted word. At the point in the transcript where the word was omitted, type an asterisk followed by the omitted word. There should be no blank spaces between the asterisk and the omitted word. For example,

WHAT WAS SAID
What is this?
This a cookie.
What do you want to do with it?
I want eat it.	TRANSCRIPTION
E  What is this?
C  This *is a cookie.
E  What do you want to do with it?
C  I want *to eat it.

Omitted Bound Morphemes

A slash followed by an asterisk is used to indicate the omission of a bound morpheme in obligatory context. Type the slash, the asterisk, and the missing bound morphemes at the point in the transcript where they've been omitted. For example,

WHAT WAS SAID
Where does the boy go?
The boy go here.
Then what happened?
The frog jump off.	TRANSCRIPTION
E  Where does the boy go?
C  The boy go/*3s here.
E  Then what happen/ed?
C  The frog jump/*ed off.

Omitted Contractions: Words vs Bound Morphemes

Omitted contractions may be transcribed in two ways, as an omission of a word or as an omission of a bound morpheme. It doesn't make much difference which way you mark this type of omission since omissions are not included in any of the calculations based on words or morphemes. The important thing is to mark that the omission occurred. For example,

WHAT WAS SAID
He going home.	TRANSCRIPTION
C  He *is go/ing home.
OR
C  He/*'s go/ing home.

Omitted Word Root/Bound Morpheme Combinations

If the word root is omitted, all bound morphemes located after the word root are also omitted, whether or not they are explicitly marked. For example,

WHAT WAS SAID
One more then done.	TRANSCRIPTION
C  One more then *we/'re done.
OR
C   One more then *we/'re done.
OR
C  One more then *we/*'re done.


Omitted Something - Not Sure What

With some omissions, it's difficult to know what the speaker intended to say. In these cases, you should make a "best guess" at what omission makes the most sense in the context of the sample. Use the following guidelines to help with these decisions: 1) use omissions conservatively; do not over-penalize the speaker, 2) choose words or bound morphemes that are representative of the speaker's ability, 3) use a generic part of speech, e.g., *verb, if you know the part of speech that was omitted but not the actual word(s), and 4) if there are more than two omitted words or bound morphemes, use the [EU] code at the end of the utterance. Consider these examples where there are different options for marking omissions:

WHAT WAS SAID
And then said, "Oh I forgot to mention that".	TRANSCRIPTION
C  And then *he said, "Oh I forgot to mention that".
OR
C  And then *Dr_DeSoto said, "Oh, I forgot to mention that".
WHAT WAS SAID
It taste good.	TRANSCRIPTION
C  It taste/*3s good.
OR
C  It taste/*ed good.
WHAT WAS SAID
There was a girl named Goldilocks went into the house.	TRANSCRIPTION
C  There was a girl named Goldilocks *who went into the house .
OR
C  There was a girl named Goldilocks.
C  *She went into the house.
If you can tell the part of speech that's missing, but not the actual word(s), you can mark the omission as in the following examples:
WHAT WAS SAID
And then went home.
And he the frog.
He laughing and laughing.	TRANSCRIPTION
C  And then *SUBJ went home.
C  And he *VERB the frog.
C  He *AUX laugh/ing and laugh/ing .
There are limits to how much you should "fix" the speaker's utterance. If there are more than two omitted words or bound morphemes, or if you don't have a good idea of what was omitted, flag the utterance with the error code [EU]. The [EU] code indicates an utterance-level error. Coded utterances can be counted and called up later for further analysis.
WHAT WAS SAID
What is Sammy doing?
He book.	TRANSCRIPTION
E  What is Sammy do/ing?
C  He book [EU].
